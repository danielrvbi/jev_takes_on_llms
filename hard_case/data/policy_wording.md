## 1. Meaning of words used

**Accidental damage** means physical loss of or damage to the insured vehicle caused by a sudden, unintended external event during the period of insurance. A sequence of closely connected impacts arising from one occurrence is treated as one incident. A mechanical failure, gradual deterioration, or damage that was already present is not itself accidental damage under this definition.

**Insured vehicle** means the vehicle identified on the schedule, including standard equipment fitted by its manufacturer. It does not include personal property, loose goods, or equipment not declared to us where the wording requires declaration.

**You / your** means the policyholder and, where the relevant section says so, a driver who is covered under the schedule. It does not make every person in the household an insured driver.

**Business use** means use in connection with a trade, occupation, profession, or business, including transport of goods or passengers for payment or reward, or use to perform a paid delivery or collection. It does not include ordinary travel to and from one regular workplace where commuting is selected, nor incidental carriage of the insured's own personal belongings. A single fact such as a box in the vehicle does not by itself determine the purpose of a journey. The actual purpose and any remuneration or service obligation may be relevant.

**Wear and tear** means deterioration through ordinary use, age, weather, corrosion, fatigue, or lack of maintenance. Damage resulting from an insured accident may still be covered even if the vehicle also has wear, subject to the exclusions and the amount properly attributable to the accident.

**Prior damage** means loss or damage that happened before the event being claimed, whether or not it was reported to us, repaired, or the subject of another claim. A payment under an earlier claim does not make a later, separate accident ineligible. We will not pay twice for the same physical damage. If an accident damages a neighbouring panel that must reasonably be blended for a proper repair, we may consider a proportionate repair cost when supported by evidence; that does not automatically include restoration of an unrelated old defect.

**Reasonable repair cost** means the cost of restoring the insured vehicle, as nearly as reasonably possible, to its pre-incident condition using suitable parts and methods, taking account of age, condition, repairability, safety, and the evidence available. It does not include an upgrade or betterment unless the additional cost is unavoidable and we agree it.

**Incident** means the occurrence or connected series of occurrences from which a claim is made. Distinct events on different dates are distinct incidents even if the repair work is completed together.

## 2. Comprehensive cover

Subject to the schedule, exclusions, conditions, and excess, we will pay for accidental damage to the insured vehicle during the period of insurance. Cover applies whether or not the policyholder was at fault, but this section does not determine responsibility between drivers or guarantee recovery from another party. We may repair, replace, pay the reasonable cost of repair, or settle on another basis allowed by this wording. We decide the appropriate settlement method after considering the circumstances and available evidence.

We may arrange inspection, request photographs, obtain an independent estimate, or ask a repairer to separate work attributable to this incident from pre-existing work. A repair estimate is not an admission that every line is covered or that the amount is agreed. Work authorized by us is limited to the scope and amount stated in the authorization. An authorization to inspect or move a vehicle is not repair authority.

The insured must take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Necessary emergency work to make the vehicle safe or prevent additional damage may be undertaken without prior authority up to €400 including applicable tax, provided the insured contacts us as soon as practicable and retains itemized evidence. This limited allowance does not authorize a full repair, replacement of unrelated parts, storage without limit, or a betterment. If the vehicle is unsafe to drive, the insured should not drive it merely to avoid towing charges.

## 3. Third-party motor liability

We cover legal liability arising from the use of the insured vehicle by a driver covered under the schedule, within the policy territory and for a permitted use. Liability cover is subject to applicable law and the wording. A customer report, police incident record, insurer's provisional view, or repair authorization is not a final decision about legal liability. Where another vehicle is involved, we may investigate and communicate with the other insurer. We may consider split or disputed responsibility on the available evidence. The policyholder must not admit legal liability, offer settlement, or waive a recovery right without our written agreement. This clause does not prevent a factual, non-argumentative account of what the person recalls.

## 4. Territory, period, and use

Cover applies in Veyra and the listed neighbouring territories in the schedule. Bellweather Ring and Slate Yard access are within Veyra for purposes of this fictional policy. The event date must fall within the period shown in the schedule. The selected use permits social, domestic, pleasure, and commuting to one regular workplace. It does not provide cover for a journey undertaken as a paid courier or delivery, commercial carriage, hire, or reward. The use condition applies to the purpose of the relevant journey, not merely to the vehicle's appearance, the presence of a parcel, or the driver's occupation. If the purpose is unclear, it should be established before the exclusion is applied.

## 5. Exclusions and limits relevant to a collision claim

We do not cover:

1. damage that existed before the incident, except to the extent that a new accidental event causes a separately evidenced worsening;
2. the cost of correcting a defect, failed component, or deterioration that did not result from an insured event;
3. wear and tear, corrosion, mechanical or electrical breakdown, or gradual damage, unless a separate covered accident causes additional physical damage;
4. any amount for which the insured has already received payment or repair for the same damage, or any amount that would place the insured in a better financial or physical position than immediately before the incident, except where a reasonable repair method necessarily entails an agreed incidental improvement;
5. loss arising while the insured vehicle is being used outside the use declared on the schedule, including a paid delivery or carriage for reward;
6. damage while the vehicle is driven by a person who is not an eligible listed driver, unless an express extension shown on the schedule applies;
7. loss deliberately caused by the policyholder or a covered driver, or a claim materially based on a statement the person knows to be false. An inconsistency, error, or missing record is not by itself proof of deliberate conduct;
8. the excess shown on the schedule, any voluntary excess shown separately, and any amount exceeding a section limit.

The exclusions are applied to the facts of the claim and the wording. An uncertain fact is not treated as established solely because it appears in one preliminary note. If the information available supports more than one reasonable account, the handler should record what is known, what is reported, and what still needs to be checked.

## 6. Driver conditions

Only Nera Vale and Iven Rusk are listed drivers on the supplied schedule. The policy does not include an “any driver” extension. A person may be a passenger without being a driver for the purpose of this condition. If there is conflicting evidence about who controlled the vehicle, the handler should clarify the point rather than infer the driver from ownership, an email signature, or who contacted the insurer. A driver's name being mentioned in a police record or repair instruction does not alone show that the person was driving.

A driver must have a valid licence and must not drive while disqualified, impaired, or otherwise prohibited. No evidence in this packet establishes impairment or an invalid licence. This policy extract is not a finding about either matter.

## 7. Notification, evidence, and cooperation

The policyholder must tell us about an incident as soon as reasonably practicable and normally within seven calendar days. If a delay occurs, the insured should explain it; notification after seven days does not automatically decide cover. The policyholder should provide a truthful account, contact details for involved parties and witnesses where available, photographs, police or incident reference information, receipts, repair estimates, and other reasonably requested material. We may request originals or a clearer copy if a document is unreadable or incomplete.

The policyholder must cooperate with reasonable enquiries, permit an inspection, preserve damaged parts where reasonably possible, tell us before agreeing non-emergency repairs, and allow us to pursue or defend recovery rights. Cooperation does not require the insured to guess, adopt another person's account, or certify facts outside their knowledge. If a record cannot be obtained, the insured should say so and explain why. We should distinguish failure to provide a document from evidence that the document never existed.

Unless emergency work is necessary under section 2, do not dispose of damaged parts or authorize a full repair before we have a reasonable chance to inspect or agree an alternative evidence method. We may consider reasonable steps taken in good faith where advance contact was not practicable. The person handling the claim should document the timing and scope of any work already done.

## 8. Claims settlement and documentation

We may ask for a detailed estimate with separate labour, parts, paint, diagnostic, towing, storage, tax, and prior-damage entries. The insured should provide an invoice or proof of payment if requesting reimbursement for costs already incurred. An estimate that combines work for two incidents should identify which lines relate to each incident. A duplicate line in an estimate should be reconciled with the repairer; it should not be assumed to be a duplicate payment request or intentional inflation without further evidence.

We may use new, reconditioned, or equivalent parts appropriate to the vehicle's age and condition where lawful and technically suitable. If a part is unavailable, we may consider a reasonable alternative. Calibration work should be supported by a vehicle-specific requirement, diagnostic record, or repairer explanation. We do not pay for a calibration merely because it appears in a generic menu of services.

For repairable damage, the standard excess is deducted from the covered amount payable for this incident. If a responsible third party later reimburses us or the policyholder, the excess may be recovered in whole or in part according to the recovery outcome and policy administration rules. The excess does not apply twice to multiple line items within one incident. Separate incidents have separate excesses where each is claimed.

## 9. Information and statements

The policyholder must take reasonable care to give accurate information when entering into or changing the policy and when making a claim. We may ask a person to confirm a material statement if records conflict. A mistaken date, incomplete answer, uncertain recollection, or correction offered voluntarily is assessed in context. We will not label a claim fraudulent solely because accounts differ, a prior claim exists, a repair line is unclear, or a customer cannot locate an old document. Suspected deliberate misrepresentation must be referred through the applicable internal process before an adverse conclusion is communicated.
